The state of the ocean

The oceans were at the origin of life.

They still provide the basic conditions of our life on earth. The UN Ocean Decade turns that knowledge into a shared deadline for protection, restoration, and better choices.

The UN sees this decade as an opportunity.

By 2030, at least 30% of our oceans must be protected. The Ocean Decade is a rallying point — a shared scientific and political framework to restore ocean health before it's too late.

Beautiful oceans have these four ugly problems

Mineral extraction can disturb habitats that formed over thousands of years and are still barely understood.

Overfishing is catching fish faster than they can reproduce, driving fish stocks toward collapse and degrading marine environments. It threatens global food security and drives the loss of ocean biodiversity.

Every year 19–23 million tonnes of plastic waste leaks into aquatic ecosystems. Plastic pollution can alter natural processes, reducing ecosystems' ability to adapt to climate change

Coral reefs, sponges, and seabed ecosystems are reduced to rubble after a single trawl. Once gone, these deep habitats may take centuries to recover.
